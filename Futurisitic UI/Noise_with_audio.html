Module 01 — Introduction

What is the Fourier Series?

Any periodic function f(x) defined on the interval [c, c + 2L] can be expressed as an infinite sum of sinusoidal components — sines and cosines — each scaled by a Fourier coefficient.

f(x) = a₀/2 + Σ[aₙcos(nπx/L) + bₙsin(nπx/L)]
where n = 1, 2, 3, …, ∞  ·  interval [c, c + 2L]
a₀ = (1/L) ∫cc+2L f(x) dx
aₙ = (1/L) ∫cc+2L f(x) cos(nπx/L) dx
bₙ = (1/L) ∫cc+2L f(x) sin(nπx/L) dx
L is the half-period  ·  c is the start of the interval  ·  a₀/2 is the DC (mean) value

Module 02 — Requirements

Dirichlet's Conditions

A Fourier Series is only defined and valid when the function satisfies all of the following Dirichlet's Conditions.

CONDITIONS FOR EXISTENCE OF FOURIER SERIES
1

f(x) is a periodic function defined on the interval [c, c + 2L].

2

f(x) is finite and single valued.

3

f(x) has its integral within the given interval of x.

4

f(x) has at most a finite number of finite discontinuities in the interval of x.

5

f(x) has at most a finite number of maxima and minima in the given interval of x.

NOTE — If any one of these conditions is violated, the Fourier Series representation of f(x) does not exist. Most physically realizable signals in engineering satisfy all five conditions.

About THD %
Signal distortion analysis
▼

Total Harmonic Distortion measures how much of a signal's energy comes from harmonics (n ≥ 2) relative to the fundamental (n = 1). A lower THD means a purer, cleaner signal.

FORMULA
THD = √(a₂² + a₃² + … + aₙ²) / a₁ × 100%
QUALITY LEVELS
  • 0% THD = 0% — pure sine wave; only the fundamental is present.
  • <1% THD < 1% — excellent quality; used in precision audio and power systems.
  • 1–5% THD 1–5% — acceptable for most consumer electronics.
  • >10% THD > 10% — clearly audible distortion; typical of square and sawtooth waves.

Time vs Frequency Domain

The same signal looks completely different depending on how you look at it. Time domain shows amplitude over time; frequency domain reveals which frequencies are present.

PHASOR ANIMATION — Rotating Vectors

How Phasors Build Waves

Each harmonic is a rotating phasor (vector). The tip of each phasor traces a circle at its harmonic frequency. By chaining phasors tip-to-tail, their combined endpoint traces out the Fourier series approximation.

The green phasor is the fundamental (n=1), cyan is n=3, amber is n=5. Their sum traces the red path.

Module 06

Real-World Applications

Fourier Series is the mathematical backbone of modern signal processing — present in virtually every technology we use.

🎧

Active Noise Cancellation

Headphones sample ambient noise, compute its Fourier components, generate the exact anti-phase wave, and combine them — canceling noise at the ear.

📡

Radio & Wireless

AM/FM modulation, filtering adjacent channels, and demodulating signals all rely on isolating specific Fourier frequency components.

🏥

Medical Imaging (MRI)

MRI scanners collect data in the frequency domain (k-space). Inverse Fourier transforms reconstruct the image you see on screen.

🎵

Audio Compression (MP3)

MP3 uses Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (a Fourier variant) to discard inaudible frequency components, reducing file size by ~90%.

📸

Image Compression (JPEG)

JPEG applies DCT (Discrete Cosine Transform) to 8×8 image blocks, encoding the dominant frequency patterns while discarding the rest.

⚡

Power Grid Analysis

Harmonics in power lines cause efficiency losses. Fourier analysis identifies distorting harmonics; active filters cancel them to clean the grid.
